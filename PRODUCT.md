# Product

## Register

product

## Users

StockWinWin serves active retail traders, financially curious builders, hackathon judges, and early investors who need to evaluate whether an AI research desk can produce a repeatable paper-trading thesis log. They use it before risking capital, often with incomplete context and a need to separate market evidence from model theater.

## Product Purpose

StockWinWin turns the one-person investment bank concept into a public paper-only workflow: Macro, Research, and Risk agents produce deterministic briefs; the user confirms allocation; the system records a paper ledger and scores it against SPY. Success means repeatable research logs, clear validation history, and credible proof that the product helps users think better before any broker integration exists.

## Brand Personality

Sober, sharp, accountable. The interface should feel like a compact research workbench: serious enough for traders, understandable enough for new users, and honest about uncertainty.

## Anti-references

Do not look like an AI-purple landing page, a casino-style trading game, a magic CIO that decides everything, a broker execution console, or a generic card-heavy dashboard template. Avoid any implication of guaranteed profit, personalized financial advice, real order routing, or autonomous trading.

## Design Principles

1. Evidence before action: show what each agent believes, why, and what would invalidate it before the user allocates.
2. Human confirmation is the product boundary: agents brief and guardrail, but the user owns the allocation.
3. Paper-only must be visible in the workflow, not hidden in legal copy.
4. Score thinking, not bravado: alpha, benchmark, drawdown, and history matter more than one-day PnL.
5. Dense but calm: prioritize scanability, stable controls, and restrained visual hierarchy over spectacle.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast, visible keyboard focus, touch targets of at least 44px where practical, reduced-motion support, and color-independent state labeling. Copy should avoid financial jargon when a precise plain-English term works.
